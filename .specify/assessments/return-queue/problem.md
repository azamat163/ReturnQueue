# Problem Definition: Coordinating Returns Across Stores

- **Slug**: return-queue
- **Created**: 2026-10-03
- **Inputs used**: intake.md; research.md; user goal.

## Problem Statement

HYPOTHESIS: US shoppers with several simultaneous clothing returns expend effort
remembering what to return, where to take it, and whether the money arrived afterward.
The frequency, severity, and willingness to pay in this subgroup are unverified.

## Affected Users & Stakeholders

- **Candidate users**: US iPhone users with at least two online purchase returns in
  the previous month; this screening threshold is a proposed research choice.
- **Buyer**: the individual shopper; payer and user are expected to coincide.
- **Founder**: wants a useful project with an achievable paid offering.

## Goals

- Reduce effort coordinating returns across stores.
- Preserve the connection between a submitted item and an expected refund.
- Establish whether the benefit warrants repeat use and payment.

## Non-Goals

- Guarantee a refund or interpret consumer rights.
- Optimize all household finances or catalog all owned items.
- Encourage purchase/return abuse.

## Success Metrics

Proposed experimental thresholds, not measured facts or industry benchmarks:

- At least 5 of 10 screened interviewees describe a recent concrete coordination problem.
- At least 4 of 5 prototype testers complete a three-item scenario independently.
- Median entry time below 45 seconds per item after the first attempt.
- At least 3 of 5 testers use it for another real return cycle.
- At least 2 of 5 accept a concrete paid pilot offer; actual purchase is stronger evidence
  than verbal agreement. A tiny sample supports only a further small experiment.

Baselines are unknown; collect them from the current workflow before claiming improvement.

## Cost of Inaction

Unknown for this subgroup. Existing tools remain available. Obtain examples and amounts
from real users; do not extrapolate losses from the value of all US returns.

## Open Questions

1. Is cross-store coordination a frequent, meaningful problem?
2. Does current software already solve it adequately?
3. Is manual capture acceptable?
4. Will users return to the workflow and pay?
5. Can we reach this audience economically?
